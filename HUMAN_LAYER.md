# ALLOCATE: THE HUMAN LAYER

*A companion reading. Not an amendment to the manifesto — a second lens on the same law.*

---

**Three registers, one law: physical, monetary, temporal.**

allocate.energy's physical layer works because it takes allocation seriously — energy measured, not promised; harvested honestly, not metered into fiction. The coin-tainer work does the same at the monetary layer — custody measured and held, not trusted to someone else's ledger.

Neither of those layers builds itself. A panel doesn't decide to be installed. A coin doesn't decide to be self-custodied. Both require a person who has already made a prior shift — from allocation imposed to allocation chosen — and made it *first*, in the register that costs nothing and needs no infrastructure: their own hours.

This is why the temporal layer comes before the other two here, not alongside them. The physical and monetary layers are what conscious allocation looks like once it has capital and hardware to work with. The temporal layer is what it looks like before that — and if it doesn't hold at that level, nothing built on top of it is standing on anything real.

---

## 1. THE FIRST MOVE, REPEATABLE AT ZERO COST

Freedom is usually treated as a destination — planned for, arrived at, granted by someone else's permission.

Conscious allocation is not that.

It requires no infrastructure, no buyer, no grid, no other node agreeing to anything. It costs nothing to begin and nothing to repeat. It is available in this hour, and it will be available in the next one, unchanged.

Say it plainly, the way a mantra is said plainly:

*Measure what you have. See the constraint. Choose. Live with the result. Repeat daily.*

Say it again tomorrow. Nothing about it weakens with repetition — it makes no promise about a future state, only names an action available now.

It is also difficult to argue with, and that is not a rhetorical accident. It asserts nothing about the world that could be disputed. It does not ask for belief, agreement, or evidence. It only asks a person to act inside a constraint that already, undeniably, exists. There is no version of "no" that survives contact with it — refusing to allocate consciously is not a rebuttal, it is simply an unconscious allocation, made anyway, by default.

This is the entry price of the whole framework: zero. Everything below is not a separate set of ideas — it is what the world looks like once this first move is actually being made.

---

## 2. THE SAME LAW, TWO UNITS

The manifesto begins: *reality comes first.* Energy arrives and disappears. Time moves one direction. Materials wear, break, and decay.

That law was written for kilowatts. It was never only about kilowatts.

Nothing in nature holds an unbounded claim on energy and time. Every living thing pays thermodynamic dues: the farmer waits real seasons, the worker spends real man-hours, the miner burns real joules.

Watts and man-hours are not analogies for each other. They obey the identical rule — non-refundable, arriving at a fixed rate, spent whether allocated well or badly. A joule mismanaged and a man-hour mismanaged fail the same way: gone, without appeal.

A man-hour is not clock time. It is measured output — one worker, one hour, applied. The manifesto insists energy be measured in real units, not promised in abstraction. Labor gets the same standard, or the same lie becomes possible on both sides of the ledger.

---

## 3. TIME MOVES ONE DIRECTION, FOR PEOPLE TOO

The manifesto's irreversibility clause was written about capital and hardware. It applies with no modification to a life.

An hour spent is not stored. It is not recoverable at a discount later. There is no ledger that credits back a morning.

Systems that pretend otherwise — that treat a person's time as elastic, as always available for one more ask — are lying about a constraint as physical as depreciation.

---

## 4. ATTENTION IS ALLOCATED, NOT INFINITE

Focus does not generate reality. It generates outcome — which is a smaller, truer claim, and the one worth keeping.

What a person attends to is what gets built, maintained, or left to decay. This is not mysticism. It is the same statement the manifesto makes about capital: *capital that does not change physical reality is inert.* Attention that is not consciously spent is inert in exactly the same sense — not powerful, not wasted in some cosmic sense, just unspent, and therefore unspent on anything.

The correction worth holding onto: attention is a resource to allocate, not a force that authors the world. Keep the first claim. Discard the second.

---

## 5. THE METERING OF BOTH LAYERS

If energy is decentralized and free, the entire illusion collapses.

That sentence was written about kilowatts and a grid connection. It reads the same way about hours and a wage.

A monopoly on energy access justifies scarcity, and scarcity justifies extraction — the manifesto's own chain. A monopoly on labor access works identically: when a person's man-hours have nowhere else to go, their price is set by the buyer, not the market. Decentralized energy removes the first chokepoint. It does not automatically remove the second. That is the gap this document sits in.

---

## 6. EXTRACTION IS MISALLOCATION, NAMED HONESTLY

The manifesto has a word for capital that promises certainty it can't deliver: *dishonest.* It has a word for hidden consequence: *lying.*

Extraction of human labor is not a moral accusation requiring belief in anyone's hidden intent. It is a measurement failure — the same category of failure the manifesto assigns to a system that issues promises without inputs. When man-hours are spent and the return is not visible, not measured, not accounted for, the system is doing to labor what fiat economics does to energy: treating a real, limited, non-refundable input as if it were abstraction.

This is diagnosis, not accusation. The manifesto does not ask who is guilty. Neither does this.

---

## 7. NO CENTRAL ALLOCATOR OF A LIFE, EITHER

*Many villages. No kingdom.* — written about energy nodes, and true without edit about hours.

There is no master allocator of anyone's time. Each person decides locally what their hours are for, same as each node decides locally what its watts are for. Coordination between people, like coordination between nodes, emerges from shared measurement — honest accounting of what was spent and what it returned — not from a central authority assigning value to someone else's morning.

The first move was solitary. This is where it starts to compound: each person who makes it becomes a node other honest nodes can coordinate with, without anyone having asked permission first.

---

*This document reads a second layer into a manifesto that already contained it. Energy arrives and disappears. So does a day. The law was never only about kilowatts. It begins, for a person, the same way it begins for a village: with one honest measurement, made now — and the physical and monetary layers only ever follow from here.*
