Encumbrance Matrix

What this is

An encumbrance is anything that isn't fully your own choice — a person, institution, or condition with a claim on your time, money, or decisions. This board plots the encumbrances that fall on most adult lives, positioned by what kind of hold they have on you and how hard that hold is to loosen. The further a dot sits from the center, the more difficult it is to become free of it.

The two axes

  • Coercion vs. scarcity (left–right) — Coercion means a specific entity with its own will has power over you: it can compel you, grant you permission, or withdraw something you need. Scarcity means no one is imposing anything — a resource (time, money) is simply finite, and that finiteness constrains you regardless of anyone's intent.
  • Systemic vs. individual (top–bottom) — Systemic means the constraint comes from a structure with no alternate counterparty: you can't escape the tax system by picking a different taxman. Individual means it's a specific relationship — this landlord, this employer — that could in principle be renegotiated, ended, or replaced with a different one.
Why some dots are a blend, and the four categories

A few encumbrances pull on both axis ends at once. A credit card company has a will (coercion) but the actual mechanism it uses is engineered scarcity of money through compounding debt. These sit in their own hybrid color rather than being forced onto one side.

  • Coercion — a specific entity holding power over you.
  • Scarcity — a finite resource with no one imposing it.
  • Hybrid — coercion exercised through manufactured scarcity.
  • Dependency/obligation — something you need or owe, without either side holding power over the other (a doctor, a family relationship).

The 13 solid dots are fixed — they're close to universal for anyone in a modern economy. Add your own for anything specific to your situation (a business partner, a health condition, a custody arrangement), drag them to where they actually sit for you, and save a dated snapshot with a note. Revisited periodically, the set of snapshots becomes a record of which encumbrances you've loosened and which have crept back in.
