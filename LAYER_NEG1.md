# WHAT IT MEANS TO ALLOCATE

*Layer −1. Before hardware. Before the blog. Before anything is bought.*

---

## 1. THE DEFAULT STATE

Every hour is already being spent.

Not saved. Not paused. Spent — on habit, on obligation, on whatever asked loudest.

Most of it is spent without a decision. This is not a flaw. It is the starting condition of anyone who has never been asked to look.

The manifesto says the same thing about energy: *non-allocation is itself an allocation.* There is no neutral state. A day left undirected is not a day unspent — it is a day spent by default, on whatever filled it.

This applies before anyone reads further. It was already true this morning.

---

## 2. NOTICING

Notice the moment an hour starts, not after it's already gone.

Notice where attention goes without being asked to go there. Notice what action began before any decision was made to begin it.

No log required. No writing needed. Noticing doesn't need paper — it needs repetition, the same as everything else here. Noticed once, it means nothing. Noticed daily, a shape starts to appear on its own.

At first it feels like interrupting something automatic. That's correct. That's what noticing an automatic process feels like from inside it.

---

## 3. MEASURING

Repeat the noticing long enough and patterns surface without being written down. Repetition is the measurement.

This happens most mornings. This always follows that. This felt like a choice, and wasn't — it was a habit wearing the shape of one.

This is the stage where something changes quietly: the reader begins to see that choices were already being made, all along — just not by them. Not yet choosing differently. Just seeing, for the first time, that choosing was already happening, on autopilot, and had been the whole time.

That seeing is the raw material everything after this stage runs on.

---

## 4. CHOOSING ON PURPOSE

Once the pattern is visible, a different question becomes possible: not *where did the hour go*, but *where could it go, given what's actually true about today*.

This is the stage where noticing and measuring turn into something usable. Not a rule handed down from outside — a decision made with open eyes, using information no one else has, because no one else stood inside those hours.

It will not feel automatic. Effort is expected here, not a sign of doing it wrong. What changes isn't the difficulty of the day — the day is still finite, the same as it always was — but the fact that the finiteness is now being met on purpose, rather than absorbed by default.

This is a decision, repeated. It becomes easier the same way noticing did: not because it gets lighter, but because it gets familiar.

---

## 5. WHAT REPETITION DOES

Do it again tomorrow. And the day after.

The default state from Stage 1 does not disappear on its own — it gets replaced, slowly, by whichever pattern is repeated most. Right now that pattern is whatever filled the gap Stage 3 revealed. It can become this instead.

There is no finish line here. There is only which default wins by repetition — the old one, or the chosen one.

---

## THE MANTRA

Everything above compresses into one sentence. It will mean something different to you now than it would have at the top of this page — that's the point of reading this first.

*Measure what you have. See the constraint. Choose. Live with the result. Repeat daily.*

No investment required. No hardware. No account.

---

## IN PRACTICE

Four moves, in order:

**Measure what you have.** Not what you wish you had. The actual hours, the actual energy, the actual constraint — the same measuring from Stage 3, now applied in the moment rather than observed after the fact.

**See the constraint.** A day has what it has. A body has what it has. Pretending otherwise is the same lie the manifesto rejects at the energy layer — promises without inputs.

**Choose.** Not the perfect allocation. A decided one.

**Live with the result.** The manifesto again: *reversal always costs more than commitment.* An hour spent cannot be reclaimed at a discount later. Neither can this one.

**Review, re-measure, re-choose, refine.** The result is not the end of the cycle — it's the input to the next one. Look at what actually happened. Measure again, with what's now known. Choose again, adjusted by what the last choice taught. Nothing here is final; it isn't meant to be.

**Repeat.** Tomorrow, the same five moves, on a day that hasn't happened yet.

This is where allocate.energy actually begins.
