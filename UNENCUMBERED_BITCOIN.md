# UNENCUMBERED BITCOIN

*Companion to [ENCUMBRANCE_MATRIX.md](ENCUMBRANCE_MATRIX.md), [HUMAN_LAYER.md](HUMAN_LAYER.md) and [LAYER_NEG1.md](LAYER_NEG1.md). Tool: [tools/encumbrance_matrix.html](tools/encumbrance_matrix.html).*

> **Bitcoin only works for holders who never have to sell.**

---

## 1. The rule

**Hold in bitcoin only what you will never need to sell.**

The amount that qualifies is your **unencumbered bitcoin**:

```
Unencumbered bitcoin = bitcoin held − (every claim on your board that could force a sale)
```

Or, as a sizing rule, before buying:

```
Never-sell amount = savings − (cash for known obligations over N years + emergency buffer)
```

- **N** is the horizon. Bitcoin has had long flat or falling stretches, and other scarce assets have stalled for decades. Gold peaked in 1980 and took 28 years to regain that price. **5–10 years** is an honest N.
- Anything above the never-sell amount is not really held. It is **waiting to be sold**, and in a bad year it is the coin that gets sold at the bottom.

## 2. Why: the test that started this

A backtest from 1 January 2016 to November 2026 (daily prices; see *How to Beat the System*) tried one popular trading idea: sell a slice at every new all-time high, then buy back after a 25% fall.

- With 10% sold at each new high and the dip measured from the peak, it ended with **0.947 BTC against the holder's 1.000**.
- Measured from the sale price, it sold once in February 2017 at $1,189 and never got back in.
- Of the 48 combinations of sell % and dip % tested, **none beat holding**. The only versions that did (about +4% at best) needed a hand-tuned trigger, and their nearest neighbours lost up to 10%.

Holding won because the holder was never out of the market when the big moves came. That has a condition, though: **the holder must be able to stay in.** Over the long run, the price is not what loses money; a forced sale is. This document is about that condition.

## 3. Encumbrances are forced sellers

Every encumbrance on the matrix is a claim that can turn into a forced sale:

| Encumbrance (from the matrix) | How it forces a sale |
|---|---|
| Taxman | Tax bills, including tax on gains from earlier sales; inheritance tax |
| Landlord / bank | Rent, mortgage renewal, a loan called in, liquidation of borrowing secured on BTC |
| Credit card companies | Short-term debt that compounds while you hold |
| Insurance requirements | Premiums, excesses, uninsured events |
| Doctor / health | Illness, care, loss of earning capacity |
| Spouse/partner with legal claim | Separation, divorce settlement |
| Family / social obligations | Children, parents, weddings, funerals |
| Boss | Job loss: income stops, costs don't |
| Inflation | Costs rise faster than the cash buffer |
| Regulatory / licensing, immigration / citizenship | Fees, compliance, forced relocation |

The **Resources** list (time, calendar, health/energy, knowledge/skills, physical assets) is what you have for meeting these claims *without* touching bitcoin. The stronger the resources column, the larger the unencumbered amount can be.

**Unencumbered bitcoin is the Unencumbered Score expressed in sats.**

## 4. Applying it

1. **Plot your board.** Use the personal overlay of the [Encumbrance Matrix tool](tools/encumbrance_matrix.html). List every claim that could reach you within N years.
2. **Cost the claims.** Put a fiat figure on each: known costs (rent, tax, school), plus a buffer for the unknown ones (health, job, family).
3. **Cover them outside bitcoin.** Cash, income, land, skills. Anything already covered there is off the board.
4. **What is left is your ceiling.** Bitcoin up to that amount is unencumbered. Bitcoin above it is borrowed against your future self.
5. **Review.** Like the Unencumbered Score, this is a recurring check, not a one-off answer. The board changes with age, health and family. *Measure what you have. See the constraint. Choose. Live with the result. Review daily. Repeat.*

## 5. What this rule is not

- **Not HODL or diamond hands.** Those are attitudes; this is arithmetic. Conviction does not pay the landlord.
- **Not "only invest what you can afford to lose."** That is about loss. This is about *timing*: you can afford a 70% drawdown and still be forced to sell into it.
- **Not sizing by nerve.** "If volatility keeps you up at night, your position is too big" measures how much fear you can stand. Someone with iron nerves and a mortgage renewal next year still fails this test.
- **Not "never sell your bitcoin."** That is a commandment. This is the condition that makes the commandment possible.
- **Not "buy, borrow, die."** Borrowing against bitcoin instead of selling does not remove an encumbrance; it **adds one**, the lender, with a liquidation trigger that fires in exactly the market where you least want to sell. A loan moves the forced sale to a different place; it does not get rid of it.

## 6. The proof case

Michael Saylor's line is "You do not sell your Bitcoin." In 2026 his company, Strategy, sold bitcoin to fund dividends on its preferred stock: **1,638 BTC in one week of July–August 2026**, cutting its holdings to 842,138 BTC ([The Block, 3 Aug 2026](https://www.theblock.co/post/410451/michael-saylor-says-never-sold-his-personal-bitcoin-strategy-dumps-more-btc)).

Saylor's reply: *"I speak as one saver to another. I have never sold mine… Strategy is a public company, not my wallet."*

That is this rule stated by its loudest opponent. **The saver is unencumbered; the company is not.** Saylor himself argued that if the market believed Strategy would never sell, credit rating agencies would conclude it is not an asset ([Cointelegraph, May 2026](https://cointelegraph.com/news/strategy-mstr-michael-saylor-bitcoin-sell-statement-btc)). Once an entity has creditors and payouts, "never sell" is a slogan, not a policy.

Maximalism without being unencumbered is larping.

## 7. The market picture

- Holders who are unencumbered create real scarcity. Long-term-holder supply reached a record **~14.7M BTC in June 2026** (Glassnode, via [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/record-bitcoin-holder-supply-early-bottom-swan-ceo)), roughly three-quarters of circulating supply.
- But "long-term holder" only means coins unmoved for about **155 days**. It counts inertia, losses and break-even sellers alongside true never-sellers. Encumbered holders look identical to unencumbered ones until a bill arrives.
- The 2024–25 cycle saw one of the largest releases of long-term supply on record. Many experienced holders turned out to have a price, or an obligation.
- **A tight float plus flat demand does not push the price up; it makes it go sideways.** Unencumbered holders are what let a holder wait through those years.

## 8. The last encumbrance

Death is on everyone's board. It arrives with heirs, inheritance tax and keys, all at once. An unencumbered plan needs a **succession layer**: who inherits, how the keys pass, and how any tax is paid *without* selling the coins.

Which leads to the one category of bitcoin guaranteed never to be sold:

> **Bitcoin: lost, but never sold.**

Several million coins are thought to be lost for good. They are the most unencumbered bitcoin there is, and the strongest holders in the market. It's a joke, then a description, then a warning: make sure your coins are unencumbered for a better reason.
